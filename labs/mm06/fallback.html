Part 1: How fast does demand fall when price rises? (8 min)

The model is a logit: each shopper picks the one cereal, or no cereal, that gives the highest utility. A brand's average utility in a market is

$$\delta_{jt} = \alpha\, p_{jt} + (\text{brand effect})_j + \xi_{jt}$$

  • $p_{jt}$ is the price per serving of brand $j$ in market $t$.
  • $\alpha$ is the price coefficient, the slope we want: how much utility falls per dollar of price.
  • The brand effect is one constant per brand (loyalty to the brand). With it in the model, $\alpha$ is learned from the same brand's price moving across cities and quarters.
  • $\xi_{jt}$ is what shoppers see and the data do not: a store display, a local ad campaign.

Why the step uses instruments. Firms charge more where $\xi$ is high, where the brand is popular for reasons the data do not show. So high prices and high demand turn up together, and a plain regression understates how much price hurts demand. The fix is an instrument: a variable that moves price for cost reasons and has nothing to do with $\xi$. The data come with 20 of them, and the step names all 20 explicitly. You do not need the econometrics for the write-up; you need the sign and the size of $\alpha$.

Predict before you run. What sign will the price coefficient $\alpha$ have?

  • About zero: price does not matter much for cereal
  • Positive: a higher price signals a better cereal

Part 3: From elasticity to markup (12 min)

§11.5: a profit-maximizing firm sets $MR = MC$. With $MR = P(1 + 1/\varepsilon_D)$ this rearranges to

$$L = \frac{P - MC}{P} = -\frac{1}{\varepsilon_D}.$$

Read it backwards: if you know the elasticity, you know the markup, without ever seeing costs. That is how economists measure market power from demand data. The formula is for a firm that sells one product. Step 3a uses it as if every brand were its own firm.
