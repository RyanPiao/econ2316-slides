Perfect Competition · Practice MC #1

Q1. Which is NOT one of the four conditions defining perfect competition?

A) Many small firms, each a price-taker.

B) A homogeneous (identical) product.

C) Free entry and exit in the long run.

D) Each firm faces a downward-sloping demand curve.

Answer

D

A competitive firm is a price-taker: it faces a horizontal (perfectly elastic) demand curve at the market price. A downward-sloping firm demand curve signals market power (Ch 11).

10
Perfect Competition · Practice MC #2

Q2. Market supply in a perfectly competitive industry is found by:

A) adding up the prices each firm charges.

B) horizontally summing firm supply curves (adding quantities at each price).

C) vertically summing firm marginal costs.

D) multiplying one firm's price by the number of firms.

Answer

B

Market supply is the horizontal sum: at each price, add the quantities, giving \(Q^s = N q^*(p)\). Vertical summing is for public goods (Test 1 / later), not market supply.

Perfect Competition · Practice MC #3

Q3. In long-run competitive equilibrium, the market price equals:

A) minimum average variable cost.

B) minimum average total cost.

C) marginal revenue minus marginal cost.

D) the monopoly price.

Answer

B

Free entry drives economic profit to zero, which pins \(p^{LR} = \min ATC\). At that price each firm produces its \(\min ATC\) output and earns zero economic profit.

Perfect Competition · Practice MC #4

Q4. Each identical firm has \(\min ATC = \$12\) at output \(q^* = 10\). Market demand is \(Q^d = 2000 - 50p\). The long-run number of firms \(N^*\) is:

A) 50

B) 100

C) 140

D) 200

Answer

C

\(p^{LR} = \min ATC = 12\). \(Q^d(12) = 2000 - 50(12) = 1400\). \(N^* = Q^d/q^* = 1400/10 = 140\) firms.

Perfect Competition · Practice MC #5

Q5. The First Welfare Theorem says that a competitive equilibrium:

A) distributes income fairly.

B) maximizes total surplus (is allocatively efficient).

C) guarantees every firm a positive profit.

D) eliminates all fixed costs.

Answer

B

At \((P^*, Q^*)\), \(P^d = P^s\), so \(dTS/dQ = 0\) — total surplus is maximized. It says nothing about fairness; a binding tax, floor, or monopoly breaks it and creates DWL.

Set-up Every firm has \(c(q) = 0.5q^2 + 2q + 50\), so \(MC = q + 2\) and \(\min ATC = \$12\) at \(q = 10\). Market demand is \(Q^d = 2000 - 50p\).

Show your work for each part:

Monopoly · Practice MC #1

Q1. A monopolist faces demand \(P = 100 - Q\). Its marginal revenue curve is:

A) \(MR = 100 - Q\)

B) \(MR = 100 - 2Q\)

C) \(MR = 50 - Q\)

D) \(MR = 100 - 0.5Q\)

Answer

B

For linear demand \(P = a - bQ\), \(MR = a - 2bQ\) — same intercept, twice the slope. Here \(MR = 100 - 2Q\). Always derive \(MR\) from \(TR = P \cdot Q\).

Monopoly · Practice MC #2

Q2. With demand \(P = 100 - Q\) and constant \(MC = 20\), the monopolist's profit-maximizing quantity and price are:

A) \(Q_M = 80\), \(P_M = 20\)

B) \(Q_M = 40\), \(P_M = 60\)

C) \(Q_M = 50\), \(P_M = 50\)

D) \(Q_M = 40\), \(P_M = 40\)

Answer

B

\(MR = MC\): \(100 - 2Q = 20 \Rightarrow Q_M = 40\). Then \(P_M = 100 - 40 = 60\). (Canonical anchor: \(P = 100 - Q\), \(MC = 20\) → monopoly \(Q = 40, P = 60\).)

11
Monopoly · Practice MC #3

Q3. The Lerner index \(L = (P - MC)/P\) equals:

A) the price elasticity of demand.

B) \(-1/\varepsilon_D\) — the inverse of the (absolute) elasticity of demand.

C) marginal cost divided by price.

D) the deadweight loss.

Answer

B

\(L = (P - MC)/P = -1/\varepsilon_D\). Since \(\varepsilon_D < 0\), \(L > 0\). Smaller \(|\varepsilon_D|\) (more inelastic) means a larger markup.

11
Monopoly · Practice MC #4

Q4. Continuing Q2 (\(P = 100 - Q\), \(MC = 20\)), the competitive benchmark is \(P = MC = 20 \Rightarrow Q_C = 80\). The deadweight loss from monopoly is:

A) \$400

B) \$800

C) \$1{,}600

D) \$0

Answer

B

\(DWL = \tfrac{1}{2}(P_M - MC)(Q_C - Q_M) = \tfrac{1}{2}(60 - 20)(80 - 40) = \tfrac{1}{2}(40)(40) = \$800\).

11
Monopoly · Practice MC #5

Q5. A profit-maximizing monopolist will never choose a quantity where demand is inelastic (\(|\varepsilon_D| < 1\)) because:

A) marginal cost would be negative.

B) marginal revenue would be negative, so cutting output would raise revenue and lower cost.

C) the Lerner index would exceed 1.

D) price would be below marginal cost.

Answer

B

When \(|\varepsilon_D| < 1\), \(MR < 0\). Reducing output then raises revenue and lowers cost — so the optimum always lies in the elastic region (\(|\varepsilon_D| > 1\)).

Show your work for each part:

a

Write the marginal revenue curve. Find \(Q_M\) and \(P_M\).

b

Compute the monopolist's profit.

Pricing with Market Power · Practice MC #1

Q1. Price discrimination requires two conditions:

A) constant marginal cost and many buyers.

B) market power and the ability to prevent arbitrage (resale).

C) a homogeneous product and free entry.

D) identical consumers and a single price.

Answer

B

Without market power a firm can't set price above \(MC\); without preventing resale, low-price buyers would arbitrage and resell to high-price buyers. Both conditions are required.

A) output falls and deadweight loss rises.

B) output rises to the competitive level, consumer surplus falls to zero, and DWL is eliminated.

Pricing with Market Power · Written Problem: Third-Degree PD + Two-Part Tariff

Set-up Part 1: A monopolist with \(MC = \$8\) sells in two separable markets — adults (\(\varepsilon_A = -2\)) and students (\(\varepsilon_S = -4\)). Part 2: A tennis club's member has demand \(q = 20 - p\) for court-hours, \(MC = \$4\).

Show your work for each part:

a

Use \(P_i = MC/(1 + 1/\varepsilon_i)\) to find each group's price. Which group pays more, and why?

b

Compute the Lerner index for each group and confirm \(L_i = -1/\varepsilon_i\).

c

For the tennis club: find the optimal per-unit price and the membership fee \(F = CS(MC)\).

d

How much total profit does the club earn per member?

Answer Sketch

Oligopoly · Written Problem: Cournot & the Cartel

Set-up A duopoly faces market demand \(P = 100 - Q\) (\(Q = q_1 + q_2\)). Both firms have constant marginal cost \(MC = 20\).

Show your work for each part:

a

Cournot. Derive firm 1's best response and solve the symmetric Nash equilibrium: \(q^*\), \(Q\), \(P\), each firm's profit.

b

Cartel. Find joint-monopoly total \(Q\), \(P\), and each firm's profit. Compare to Cournot.

c

Cheating. From the cartel split in (b), firm 1 moves to its best response while firm 2 holds its cartel quantity. Find \(q_1\) and firm 1's profit, and say whether the cartel is a Nash equilibrium.

Game Theory · Practice MC #1

Q1. A dominant strategy is one that:

A) is best only if the opponent cooperates.

B) gives the player the highest payoff regardless of what the opponent does.

C) is the same as the Nash equilibrium in every game.

D) minimizes the opponent's payoff.

Answer

B

A dominant strategy is a best response to every opponent action — always play it. A dominated strategy is worse no matter what, so it can be eliminated (IESDS).

Game Theory · Practice MC #2

Q2. A Nash equilibrium is an outcome in which:

A) total payoffs are maximized.

B) each player is playing a best response to the others, so no one wants to unilaterally deviate.

C) both players have a dominant strategy.

D) the payoffs are equal.

Answer

B

At a Nash equilibrium every player best-responds to the others, so no one gains by deviating alone. It need not maximize total payoffs (the Prisoner's Dilemma is the classic counterexample).

Top Mistakes to Avoid (1–6)

1

Using \(p = MC\) for a price-maker. Price-takers (Ch 10) use \(p = MC\). Monopolists/oligopolists (Ch 11–14) use \(MR = MC\), with \(MR < P\). The #1 Test 2 error.

2

Forgetting "twice the slope" for MR. For \(P = a - bQ\), \(MR = a - 2bQ\), not \(a - bQ\). Always derive \(MR\) from \(TR = P \cdot Q\).

3

Confusing shutdown and exit. Shutdown (SR) uses \(\min AVC\); exit (LR) uses \(\min ATC\). With \(\min AVC < p < \min ATC\), operate in SR but exit in LR.

4

Treating quasi-rent as profit. \(QR = pq - VC\); profit \(= pq - VC - FC\). A firm can have positive quasi-rent and negative profit — and rationally keep operating.

5

Summing supply curves vertically. Market supply is the horizontal sum: add quantities at each price, giving \(Q^s = N q^*(p)\). Vertical summing is for public goods.

6

Lerner/elasticity sign backwards. \(L = (P - MC)/P = -1/\varepsilon_D\). Since \(\varepsilon_D < 0\), \(L > 0\). Less-elastic demand (smaller \(|\varepsilon_D|\)) → larger markup.

Top Mistakes to Avoid (7–12)

7

Monopolist in the inelastic region. A profit-maximizer always operates where \(|\varepsilon_D| > 1\); otherwise \(MR < 0\) and cutting output raises revenue while lowering cost.

8

Wrong PD group pays more. In 3rd-degree PD the inelastic segment pays the higher price (adults > students; business > tourists).

9

Forgetting the cartel is unstable. Cartel gives highest joint profit, but each member's \(MR > MC\) at the cartel quantity, so each wants to cheat — Cournot, not cartel, is the non-cooperative prediction.

10

Mis-handling mixed-strategy logic. You pick your probabilities to make the opponent indifferent, not yourself. Set the opponent's two pure-strategy payoffs equal and solve.

11

Forgetting to find ALL Nash equilibria. Use the check method on every cell. A game can have zero (matching pennies), one (PD), or several (coordination) pure-strategy equilibria.

12

Reading the \(\delta\) threshold backwards. A high \(\delta\) (close to 1) means patient players — that's what sustains cooperation. It holds when \(\delta \geq \delta^* = (T-R)/(T-P)\).

Day-of-Test Game Plan

1. Price-taker or price-maker?

The first question on every firm problem. Price-taker → \(p = MC\). Price-maker → \(MR = MC\), then read \(P\) off demand. Get this right and half the work is done.

2. Name the model before solving.

Monopoly? Cournot? Cartel? A 2×2 game? Naming the structure tells you which formula to reach for — the algebra follows.

3. For games, use the check method.

Circle each player's best response to every rival action. A cell with both circled is a Nash equilibrium. Check all cells — there can be zero, one, or several.

4. Memorize the canonical anchor.

\(P = 100 - Q\), \(MC = 20\): monopoly/cartel \((40, 60)\), Cournot \((53.3, 46.7)\). A free sanity check on any oligopoly solve.

5. Check signs & the elastic region.

\(\varepsilon_D < 0\), so \(L = -1/\varepsilon_D > 0\). A monopolist sits where \(|\varepsilon_D| > 1\). Mix to make the opponent indifferent. Easy points lost on flipped signs.

Mindset

Test 2 rewards structural reasoning: classify the market (price-taker vs. price-maker), name the model, apply its decision rule, then turn the crank. The mechanics are mechanical once the structure is right.
