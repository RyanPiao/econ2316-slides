How the Course Works

Flipped, active, and built on a predictable weekly rhythm

The big idea: a calculus-based sequel to 1116

Same questions, sharper answers. 1116 showed that a monopolist sets a markup — the direction. Here we put a number on it: the Lerner index ties that markup directly to demand elasticity, and we use it on real firms. We use the results, we don’t prove them — same topics as 1116, taken from which way to how much.

We formalize the two halves of every market —

Consumer side

preferences → utility → demand, via constrained optimization (the Lagrangian).

Producer side

production → cost → supply, via the same machinery and the envelope theorem.

…then carry that toolkit through every market structure — competition, monopoly, oligopoly, game theory — and into where markets break down: uncertainty, asymmetric information, externalities, and behavioral economics.

Prerequisite — but don’t stress: you’ll want comfort with single-variable calculus, and we’ll build up partial derivatives and the Lagrangian together. I’ll review every piece of math we actually need as we go — you don’t have to walk in with all of it. Math Appendix A and the Calculus Corner sidebars back you up; just talk to me in Week 1 if you’re unsure.

A typical day: a flipped, active classroom

You meet new ideas before we meet, so live time goes to derivations on the board, worked examples, and the kind of arguing that makes the math click.

 Before class

  • Listen to the short Pre-Lecture podcast
  • Read the chapter in the free ebook
  • Take a 5-question Canvas quiz (2 attempts, highest kept)

 In class

  • Open with a quick poll

  • Re-work the chapter’s Walkthroughs in the ebook

Yes, it’s steady work — the structure is designed so the load is predictable rather than one giant push.

How to do well (the honest version)

Plan for roughly 12 hours/week outside class. That is what a 4-credit course expects, and three 65-minute meetings are only a small part of it. The structure makes those hours predictable — not lighter.

  • Read actively — redo the Calculus Corner derivations on paper; don’t move on until you can re-derive the headline result without the book.
  • Show up & engage — the in-class derivations are where math becomes intuition (and the NUID check-in in each meeting’s polls is your participation grade).
  • Drill the Walkthroughs — the worked examples in the ebook are where the technique actually sticks, and they are the closest thing to exam practice.
  • Show your work — written homework grades the setup; a wrong number with a clean derivation beats a right number from a black box.
  • Use AI as a sparring partner — AI Labs grade your prompts as much as the output. Verbatim Claude answers read as low-effort.

How attendance works — and the polls in these slides

 Attendance = the check-in in the polls

In each meeting’s polls you enter your NUID once, then vote on your phone — one check-in per class, 35 across the term. There is no Canvas poll and no access code.

It’s graded on completion, not accuracy — any honest response earns full credit. Your lowest 3 drop automatically, so a missed class or two is already covered.

 The polls inside these slides

The quick polls built into the slides (like the next one) are not graded and are not your attendance.

They’re here to keep you actively participating — and to let me see, live, where the class stands so I can slow down or move on accordingly.
